The Philippine Revolution began in August 1896 after the Katipunan, a secret society founded by Andres Bonifacio in 1892, was discovered by Spanish authorities. Members tore their cedulas, or community tax certificates, in the Cry of Pugad Lawin to mark their break from Spain.
Jose Rizal was not a member of the Katipunan. He favored reform over armed revolt and wrote the novels Noli Me Tangere and El Filibusterismo, which exposed abuses by the friars and colonial officials. He was executed by firing squad in Bagumbayan on December 30, 1896.
The Tejeros Convention of 1897 elected Emilio Aguinaldo as president of the revolutionary government, defeating Bonifacio. The conflict between the Magdalo and Magdiwang factions ended with Bonifacio's execution in May 1897.
The Pact of Biak-na-Bato in December 1897 was a truce: Aguinaldo went into exile in Hong Kong in exchange for payment from Spain. Fighting resumed in 1898 during the Spanish-American War, and Philippine independence was proclaimed in Kawit, Cavite on June 12, 1898.
